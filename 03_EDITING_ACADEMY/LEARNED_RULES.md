## HIGH CONFIDENCE
*(Measured across the research corpus or directly observed in multiple references.)*

### H1. Open with the dated paradox: exact date, confident system one line, catastrophe one line.
- **Evidence:** Transcript-verified cold opens (FH Skyway: "On the 9th of May, 1980… at 7:33 am…"); description-proxy cold opens (Salvage Room, Hydraulic Record); exact-time anchoring in 8/10 top outliers. Zero winners open chronologically or with narrator introduction.
- **Why it matters:** The gap between the confident system and the catastrophe is the entire retention device.
- **Where it applies:** 0:00–0:20 of every episode.
- **Potential exception:** In-medias-res primary audio (distress call) as the cold open — observed once (Brick Immortar Scandies Rose); date-anchor must follow immediately.

### H2. Bank the toll within the first 60 seconds.
- **Evidence:** Salvage Room Fitzgerald 00:30 "29 Dead, No Bodies Ever Recovered"; Estonia 00:35 "852 Dead in 35 Minutes" [OBSERVED chapter maps].
- **Why it matters:** Stakes before context. The viewer decides in the first minute whether the story is worth their time.
- **Where it applies:** 0:20–1:00, flatly stated, no stinger.
- **Potential exception:** None observed. Even the 73-minute El Faro banks stakes early via its verdict title.

### H3. Narration: third-person, ~145–155 WPM, ~12-word breath units, restrained adjectives.
- **Evidence:** Measured across 14 transcripts: third-person dominant 14/14; median 146.7 WPM; 12/14 at 9–16 caption segments/min with ~12 words/segment; 1 exclamation mark total; dramatic adjectives ≤1.6/1k in 10/14.
- **Why it matters:** In this genre, narration quality is the credibility layer — competitors brand explicitly against synthetic/low-quality narration.
- **Where it applies:** All voiceover, every episode.
- **Potential exception:** The mechanism beat may run slower (Alertometer 134.6 WPM under continuous animation); the adjective ceiling may lift when the subject's horror earns it (Plainly Difficult 4.1/1k on the Demon Core — topic-licensed, not a default).

### H4. The skeleton is rewind-and-rebuild: cold open → confident system → links → warnings → point of no return → catastrophe → reckoning → legacy.
- **Evidence:** 90% of chapter-mapped videos (9/10) follow this skeleton [OBSERVED].
- **Why it matters:** It's the genre's grammar. Deviating without reason reads as amateur, not original.
- **Where it applies:** Full-episode structure.
- **Potential exception:** Countdown/real-time variant for timestamped subjects (30%); anthology only for genuine compilations (10%).

### H5. A re-hook is mandatory at 65–80%: re-open the central question (new evidence, reversed theory, vindicated warner).
- **Evidence:** Fitzgerald "Why Did She Sink?" at 80%; Estonia 2020 ROV reversal at 68%; Banqiao Chen Xing at 85%; South Tower thesis restated at 65% [all OBSERVED chapter maps].
- **Why it matters:** The catastrophe is over; attention needs a new question. The final third is the observed drop-risk zone.
- **Where it applies:** Post-catastrophe, pre-/early-reckoning, exactly once per video.
- **Potential exception:** None — if the record supports no re-hook form, the reckoning itself is framed as the question. Never manufactured.

### H6. Close on legacy + remembrance. Never on spectacle.
- **Evidence:** "Cold Open" → "Accountability"/"Remembrance"/"Conclusion" as the observed closing grammar (Hydraulic Record ×2, Trace the Failure); "This Led To The Sterile Cockpit Rule" as FH's #1 video.
- **Why it matters:** The legacy frame is the subscription engine ("this is why your world works this way") and the observed emotional engine is competence satisfaction.
- **Where it applies:** Final 2–4 minutes of every episode.
- **Potential exception:** None observed among winners.

### H7. Visual hierarchy: primary evidence > annotated diagram > cutaway > disclosed reconstruction > map stand-in > stock.
- **Evidence:** Derived from the 10 observed editing techniques ranked by evidential weight: archival-photo pipelines (FH 4.66M), annotated diagrams (Plainly Difficult red circle; USCSB live counter), mechanism cutaways (Alertometer 5.98M), disclosed reconstructions (Trace the Failure; Meridian Zero), satellite stand-ins (Banqiao GEO-KOMPSAT) [all OBSERVED].
- **Why it matters:** The genre's visual promise is evidence, not spectacle. Every level down the hierarchy costs credibility.
- **Where it applies:** Every visual decision.
- **Potential exception:** The hidden-mechanism beat legitimately inverts levels 1–3 (the cutaway *is* the evidence when the failure is invisible).

### H8. Chapters every 1–2 minutes, titled as beats; chapter titles are a second title track.
- **Evidence:** Chapter density ≈ 1 per 1–2 min across chaptered videos (Salvage Room 24/15:47; Teton 15/22:54; Banqiao 18/22:17) [OBSERVED]. The Bible mandates it; the research supports it.
- **Why it matters:** Chapters segment attention, enable the rewind grammar, and function as micro re-hooks.
- **Where it applies:** Every episode.
- **Potential exception:** Proven sustainable without chapters at long-form extreme (El Faro 73 min, no chapters) — but that's the exception, not the model.

### H9. AI reconstruction disclosed on screen, never passed off as archival. Narration must pass the documentary-ear test.
- **Evidence:** Trace the Failure's explicit VISUAL AND AUDIO DISCLOSURE [OBSERVED]; Meridian Zero's "visibly labelled dramatized reconstructions" [OBSERVED]; Brick Immortar's "ZERO AI tools" and DisastersUncovered's "No AI / Human Narrated" as brand claims [OBSERVED]. The 26.2 seg/min / 5.4 words-per-segment choppy-cadence tell measured on disclosed-AI narration [OBSERVED].
- **Why it matters:** The genre's trust fault line is AI. Undisclosed synthetic visuals are brand-killing; identifiable synthetic narration fails the credibility layer.
- **Where it applies:** Every synthetic visual; every narration render.
- **Potential exception:** None. The label is non-negotiable.

### H10. Disclose uncertainty on screen. Never manufacture a gap, a cause, or a timestamp.
- **Evidence:** Banqiao's "show you exactly how and why rather than pick one for effect" [OBSERVED]; 2/20 videos disclose disputed numbers and both are trust outliers (creative report Q9).
- **Why it matters:** In a genre built on "what really happened," admitting what isn't known is a differentiator — and manufacturing is the fastest way to destroy the channel.
- **Where it applies:** Disputed tolls, unresolved mechanisms, thin records, absent warnings.
- **Potential exception:** None.

## MEDIUM CONFIDENCE
*(Supported by structural evidence or strong inference, but not directly measured. Promote on playback/retention evidence.)*

### M1. The catastrophe is the slowest-cut passage: decelerate, longer holds, silence or near-silence.
- **Evidence:** Structural inference from the restraint norm + the "empty radar screen" silence-shaped chapter (Salvage Room 09:20) [INFERRED]. Supported by the pace-as-meaning logic, not by measured cut rates.
- **Why it matters:** Spectacle is decelerated; the chain is accelerated. Fast cuts on the disaster read as spectacle-mongering.
- **Where it applies:** The catastrophe beat, every episode.
- **Potential exception:** Countdown subjects where the clock demands continuity — even then, holds lengthen relative to the escalation.

### M2. Re-escalate after every context block: warning-sign material goes immediately after history sections.
- **Evidence:** Sayano's structure — history block then "Rough Zone" → "Resonance" → "Fatigue" chapters placed exactly where attention would sag [OBSERVED structure; retention effect INFERRED].
- **Why it matters:** The 26–30% background-before-disaster stretch is the observed risk zone.
- **Where it applies:** After any context block exceeding ~2 minutes.
- **Potential exception:** Context blocks under 90 seconds may not need it.

### M3. The point of no return is named on screen, as a chapter and a graphic beat, followed by 2–3 seconds of near-stillness.
- **Evidence:** Estonia 08:35 "Past the Point of No Return" [OBSERVED]; Scandies Rose 15:14 "Vanishing Angle — rollover minutes away" [OBSERVED]. The stillness prescription is inference.
- **Why it matters:** The hinge between *decisions* and *physics*. The viewer must feel the trap close.
- **Where it applies:** End of escalation, every episode.
- **Potential exception:** If the record supports no defensible point, name the window honestly rather than inventing precision.

### M4. The parallel-case interlude (one related failure, 60–150s, placed late or mid-escalation) trains catalog thinking.
- **Evidence:** 30% of chaptered videos include one (Fontenelle at Teton 20:15; F/V Destination at Scandies Rose 08:00) [OBSERVED]. The session/retention benefit is [INFERENCE].
- **Why it matters:** The next video is teased inside this one; the channel becomes an interconnected catalog.
- **Where it applies:** Maximum once per video, only when the parallel is genuinely defensible.
- **Potential exception:** Episodes where no honest parallel exists — never force it.

### M5. Annotated diagrams are the signature layer: ≤3 marks per image, marks land on nouns, flat red technical style.
- **Evidence:** Plainly Difficult's red-circle thumbnail and USCSB's live counter [OBSERVED]. The ≤3-marks and sync rules are production inference from the clutter principle.
- **Why it matters:** This is our visual identity inside the video (Bible §6.1). Consistency here is brand.
- **Where it applies:** Every mechanism and link beat.
- **Potential exception:** Complex systems may need a sequence of diagrams rather than one marked-up image.

### M6. The micro-cause beat: show the small thing at human scale, then hard-cut to the macro-consequence.
- **Evidence:** 9/10 small-channel breakouts use the micro-cause → macro-consequence frame in packaging [OBSERVED]. The edit-level prescription (tight shot → hard cut → consequence) is inference from the device's logic.
- **Why it matters:** The most portable breakout device in the research, translated from title/thumbnail into editing.
- **Where it applies:** Link beats where a small decision had disproportionate consequence.
- **Potential exception:** Systemic failures with no clean micro-cause — never manufacture one.

### M7. Document beats: one document, one push-in, one highlighted passage, 8–15 seconds, near-silence.
- **Evidence:** Report-material as visual spine (Meridian Zero NTSB/USCG/TSB; El Faro NTSB/DoD; Trace the Failure NIST) [OBSERVED]. Timing and silence are production inference.
- **Why it matters:** The paper trail under the chain. Stillness is the credibility signal.
- **Where it applies:** Warnings beat (the ignored memo), reckoning (the report's conclusion).
- **Potential exception:** None — but never more than ~3 document beats per video or the rhythm dies.

## EXPERIMENTAL
*(Conservative principles without direct measurement. Validate against real reference playback at the first opportunity. Do not promote without evidence.)*

### E1. Music: low or absent by default; one rising passage per video (the escalation); silence at the catastrophe; sober resolved close.
- **Evidence:** None direct [RESEARCH GAP]. Consistent with the restraint norm by extension.
- **Why it matters (hypothesis):** Music must never do the shouting the narrator refuses to do.
- **Where it applies:** Full mix, every episode. See music/MUSIC_RULES.md.
- **Potential exception / to test:** Whether the re-hook lift is real or should stay flat.

### E2. SFX whitelist: documented primary audio only. No whooshes, impacts, ticking clocks, stingers, or invented Foley — ever.
- **Evidence:** None direct [RESEARCH GAP]. The bans extend the restraint norm to the track.
- **Why it matters (hypothesis):** The genre doesn't sonify its editing; SFX would break the documentary contract.
- **Where it applies:** The entire sound design. See sound_design/SOUND_DESIGN_RULES.md.
- **Potential exception / to test:** Archival news audio beds under montage passages — needs a real reference to judge.

### E3. Shot-duration table (4–6s cruise, 8–15s documents, 45–120s cutaway, longest holds at catastrophe).
- **Evidence:** Derived from the 9–16 ideas/min caption proxy [OBSERVED proxy]; true durations unmeasured [RESEARCH GAP].
- **Why it matters (hypothesis):** Gives the editing agent concrete hold targets instead of vibes.
- **Where it applies:** Edit decision lists.
- **Potential exception / to test:** Validate against actual reference playback; adjust table on measurement.

### E4. The 90-second benchmark (≥36/60, no 0 on hook/visual-quality/sync, honesty gate) predicts episode-level editing competence.
- **Evidence:** None yet — the benchmark is new. Its dimensions are grounded in measured norms, but its predictive validity is untested.
- **Why it matters (hypothesis):** Sentence-level editing grammar (decisions, sync, restraint, honesty) is testable in 90 seconds; full-video architecture is tested by the Bible's 9 gates.
- **Where it applies:** Editing-agent evaluation before full-episode work.
- **Potential exception / to test:** Run the benchmark against 2–3 human-edited cold opens scored blind; calibrate thresholds.

### E5. The timeline graphic (chain filling link-by-link, unfilled at the promise, complete at the legacy) as the channel's signature in-video device.
- **Evidence:** Timeline strips (USCSB thumbnail) and chronology chapters (Teton 07:01) [OBSERVED]; the specific link-filling animation treatment is our synthesis, untested.
- **Why it matters (hypothesis):** Makes the editorial promise visible — the viewer watches the thesis assemble.
- **Where it applies:** Stakes (unfilled), link beats (filling), legacy (complete).
- **Potential exception / to test:** If it reads as UI clutter in practice, reduce to the two anchor appearances (promise + legacy).

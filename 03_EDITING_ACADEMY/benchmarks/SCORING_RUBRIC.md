# SCORING_RUBRIC — 90-second editing benchmark

Score each dimension 0–4. Score what is on the decision list (and in the rationale), not what you imagine the editor intended.

## 1. Hook
- 0 — No dated paradox in the first 20 seconds, or the sequence opens with narrator/channel introduction, or a spoken question opens it.
- 1 — Date or catastrophe present but the paradox structure is broken (e.g., chronological opening, hype language).
- 2 — Date + system + catastrophe present within ~30 seconds, stated acceptably flat.
- 3 — Clean dated paradox within 20 seconds: exact date, confident system one line, catastrophe one line, no intro, no question.
- 4 — As 3, plus the single opening image is instantly legible and the paradox gap is unbridgeable without watching.

## 2. Visual decision quality
- 0 — Stock montage or decorative visuals unrelated to the narration's claims.
- 1 — Visuals loosely related but hierarchy ignored (stock where archival exists; footage where a diagram was needed).
- 2 — Hierarchy mostly followed; one or two suboptimal selections.
- 3 — Hierarchy followed throughout: primary evidence first, diagrams for mechanisms, disclosed reconstruction only where needed, labeled.
- 4 — As 3, plus at least one selection that actively improves comprehension beyond what the narration says alone.

## 3. Pacing
- 0 — Fewer than 6 or more than 22 visual ideas in 90 seconds (outside any defensible band), or long dead holds.
- 1 — Noticeably too slow (slideshow feel) or too fast (restless cutting).
- 2 — Within the 9–16/min band but monotonous (no meaningful variation between beats).
- 3 — In-band with sensible variation: the stakes breathe, the chain promise moves.
- 4 — As 3, plus the pacing itself carries meaning (deceleration or acceleration matched to a beat).

## 4. Visual variety
- 0 — A single visual type for the full 90 seconds (all photos, all text cards).
- 1 — Two types with one dominating ~80%+.
- 2 — Three types represented but transitions between types feel arbitrary.
- 3 — Varied visual types (archival, diagram/map, text beat, timeline) each serving its narration.
- 4 — As 3, plus the variety follows the template's logic (image → stakes graphic → timeline → confident-system archival).

## 5. Archival usage
- 0 — No archival where the asset list provided it, or archival misused (wrong photo for the claim).
- 1 — Archival present but static-held past ~8 seconds without movement, or movement is the identical zoom on every still.
- 2 — Archival with movement; moves vary; no dead holds.
- 3 — As 2, plus moves end on the detail the narration names.
- 4 — As 3, plus a still earns a full beat (4–8 seconds on the key record image, e.g., the breach photo).

## 6. Diagram/technical explanation
- 0 — Technical nouns in the narration with no visual anchor.
- 1 — A diagram appears but doesn't show what the narration names (mismatch).
- 2 — Technical nouns anchored to a diagram or labeled visual, adequately.
- 3 — Annotations appear in narration sync (marks land on nouns); ≤3 marks per image.
- 4 — As 3, plus the diagram actively decodes an undecodable noun (the viewer couldn't have pictured it from words alone).

## 7. Motion
- 0 — No motion on stills held long, or chaotic multi-direction/fast moves.
- 1 — Motion present but arbitrary (doesn't serve the narration).
- 2 — One slow directional move per still; documents get slower push-ins.
- 3 — As 2, plus moves end on the named detail; no motion where stillness was needed.
- 4 — As 3, plus a deliberate stillness used as emphasis (the held breath).

## 8. Evidence
- 0 — Claims illustrated with unrelated or invented visuals; no sourcing.
- 1 — Some claims supported; sources unnamed.
- 2 — Key claims supported on screen; sources named in the list.
- 3 — As 2, plus at least one document or primary-source beat (push-in to the passage).
- 4 — As 3, plus uncertainty handled honestly where the ledger marks it (disputed/unknown shown as such).

## 9. Narration/visual synchronization
- 0 — Visuals change on a fixed timer unrelated to narration ideas, or never change.
- 1 — Rough correspondence but frequent mismatches (visual lags or leads the idea).
- 2 — Visuals change with ideas most of the time; marks mostly land on nouns.
- 3 — Tight sync throughout: one idea, one visual; annotations land on nouns.
- 4 — As 3, plus the sync creates meaning (the cut *is* the rewind; the hold *is* the weight).

## 10. Music
- 0 — Music competes with the voice, or a banned use (stinger on toll, triumphant swell).
- 1 — Music present but too loud or too active for the restraint norm.
- 2 — Low bed, ducks under narration, never competes — adequate.
- 3 — As 2, plus music reduces or drops under dense factual passages.
- 4 — As 3, plus the music plan shows deliberate architecture (near-silent hook, low bed, purposeful absence somewhere).

## 11. SFX
- 0 — Any banned SFX (whooshes, impacts, ticking clock, stinger on toll, invented Foley).
- 1 — SFX present but unjustified (sonified photo moves, decorative).
- 2 — No SFX at all (acceptable — the whitelist is permissive, not mandatory).
- 3 — As 2, with the rationale correctly noting why no SFX was needed.
- 4 — Whitelisted SFX used correctly (documented primary audio) with justification. (Rare — score 2/3 is the normal good.)

## 12. Silence
- 0 — No deliberate silence; every second filled with sound.
- 1 — Brief pauses that read as gaps rather than choices.
- 2 — At least one planned silence (or near-silence) at a meaningful beat.
- 3 — Silence scored at the right beat (toll, point-of-no-return equivalent, or document beat).
- 4 — As 3, plus the rationale explains what the silence does (the contrast is the effect).

## 13. Transitions
- 0 — Decorative transitions (dissolves, wipes, zooms, effects) or transition SFX.
- 1 — Mostly hard cuts with one or two unjustified effects.
- 2 — All hard cuts / simple fades; functional.
- 3 — As 2, plus transitions serve structure (the cut as rewind signal; the chapter title as the transition).
- 4 — As 3, with full consistency — the viewer never notices a transition.

## 14. Forward pull (micro re-hook)
- 0 — The 90 seconds resolve (the viewer has no reason to continue).
- 1 — A vague promise ("stay tuned") with no structural open loop.
- 2 — The chain promise stated; the timeline introduced with unfilled links.
- 3 — As 2, plus the confident-system opening ends on the first crack (the bridge into the chain).
- 4 — As 3, plus the viewer holds two explicit open loops (the toll + the unfilled chain, per the template).

## 15. Sequence ending
- 0 — Ends mid-idea or on a resolved beat that closes the video's question.
- 1 — Ends adequately but flat (no handoff energy).
- 2 — Closes on the chain promise; hands to the next block.
- 3 — As 2, with the final visual being the unfilled timeline or the first-crack image.
- 4 — As 3, plus the final 5 seconds are the most forward-pulling in the sequence (the edit leans into the video, not away).

## Scoring notes
- Score the decision list, not the render. A perfect list with no render still scores.
- The honesty gate (EDITING_BENCHMARK_90SEC.md) overrides: any failure there = benchmark FAIL regardless of points.
- Critical dimensions (1, 2, 9): a 0 on any one = FAIL regardless of total.
- When in doubt between two scores, take the lower. The benchmark is a floor, not a celebration.

# Pattern: ANNOTATED_DIAGRAM

## Purpose
Draw the failure: take a static diagram, photograph, or schematic and mark it up — circles, arrows, labels, counters — so the viewer's eye goes exactly to the failure point. This is our signature visual layer (Bible §6).

## When to use
- Any mechanism passage: the narrator names a part, a reading, a location on the structure — the diagram must show it at that moment.
- Archival experiment/technical photos where the failure point is visible but not obvious.
- Data that accumulates: counters, gauges, readings over time.

## When NOT to use
- When the mechanism needs *motion* to be understood (valve sequences, fluid paths) — that's MECHANISM_CUTAWAY, not annotation.
- When the image is evidence being examined (then hold it clean — see DOCUMENT_EVIDENCE).
- More than ~3 annotations per image (clutter kills comprehension).

## Narrative situation
The links and the hidden-mechanism beat. Anywhere the narration says a technical noun, the diagram must show it.

## Visual sequence
1. The clean diagram/photo, full frame, 1 second to register.
2. Annotations appear in narration sync: circle on the part as it's named; label fades in; counter starts accumulating.
3. The annotated state holds while the explanation completes.
4. Cut to the next annotated view or to the consequence.

## Typical duration
10–30 seconds per annotated beat. Annotations appear one at a time, ~2–3 seconds apart — never all at once.

## Motion strategy
Minimal camera motion — the *annotations* are the motion. Slow push-in optional while annotations accumulate. The eye should follow the marks, not the frame.

## Transition strategy
Hard cuts. Annotations appear with simple fades/pops (no bouncy animation — restraint).

## Sound strategy
[RESEARCH GAP.] Conservative: no SFX on annotation appearance (marks are explanation, not events).

## Common mistakes
- Annotating everything (if every part is circled, nothing is circled — maximum 3 marks).
- Annotations out of sync with narration (the circle must land on the noun, not before or after).
- Decorative annotation style (glows, 3D bevels) — the genre's annotation language is flat, red, technical.
- Using annotation where a cutaway is needed (static marks can't show flow — know the boundary).

## Chain-of-Failure application
This is the "draw the failure" treatment from the Bible: red annotations, live counters, mechanism callouts. Every link beat gets at least one annotated view. The chain timeline graphic itself is an annotated diagram (links filling in as the narration reaches them).

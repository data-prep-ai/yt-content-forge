# Pattern: ARCHIVAL_PHOTO_MOVEMENT

## Purpose
Give still photographs screen life without fabricating footage. The cost-effective standard for the photo-driven production model (Fascinating Horror's 4.66M-view pipeline [OBSERVED]).

## When to use
- Any archival photograph, map, or document that must hold the screen for more than ~3 seconds.
- The confident-system block, the context passages, the human-toll beats.

## When NOT to use
- When a diagram would explain better (mechanism passages — see ANNOTATED_DIAGRAM).
- When the photo's content is the *evidence* being examined (then hold it still — see DOCUMENT_EVIDENCE).
- Never on photos of victims in distress (restraint gate; not observed as a genre practice).

## Narrative situation
Context, background, aftermath, remembrance — anywhere the record is photographic rather than diagrammatic.

## Visual sequence
1. Full-frame still, held 1 second to register.
2. Slow directional move: push-in (toward the subject's face, the breach, the detail) or lateral pan (across a scene, along a document).
3. The move ends on the detail the narration is naming — motion and words arrive together.

## Typical duration
4–8 seconds per still. No static frame held past ~8 seconds without a move [production-model norm; INFERRED timing].

## Motion strategy
Slow, single-direction, constant-speed moves. Push-in = intimacy/escalation; pull-back = context/scale; lateral pan = survey/reading (documents, crowds, landscapes). One move per still — never combine push + pan (restraint).

## Transition strategy
Hard cuts between stills. The move *within* the still replaces the transition *between* stills.

## Sound strategy
[RESEARCH GAP.] Conservative: narration-forward; no SFX on photo moves (movement is not action — don't sonify it).

## Common mistakes
- Fast or multi-direction moves (reads as slideshow panic, not documentary).
- Zooming on faces for emotional effect (sentimentality the genre doesn't use).
- Holding a still static for 10+ seconds (dead air — the number-one amateur tell in photo-driven docs).
- Ken Burns on every still identically (vary direction; the pattern is *movement*, not *zoom*).

## Chain-of-Failure application
The confident-system block lives or dies on this pattern: 2 minutes of archival movement must feel like a world, not a slideshow. End the block's final still on the first crack (the warning, the questionable decision) — the move should land on the detail that bridges into the chain.

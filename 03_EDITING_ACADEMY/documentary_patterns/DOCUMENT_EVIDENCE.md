# Pattern: DOCUMENT_EVIDENCE

## Purpose
Put the primary source on screen: the report page, the memo, the telegram, the inspection record — filmed as evidence, not as decoration. The document close-up is a credibility beat: the viewer sees that the claim comes from paper.

## When to use
- The reckoning: investigation findings, report conclusions, inquiry quotes.
- The warnings beat: the memo that was ignored, the inspection that passed, the vibration data.
- Any disputed point: show the record's own words rather than paraphrasing (uncertainty disclosure).
- Corrections: if the record corrects itself, show the correction (Fascinating Horror's published CORRECTIONS section [OBSERVED]).

## When NOT to use
- As background texture under unrelated narration (a document on screen is a *claim* — it must match the words).
- More than ~3 document beats per video (each one must earn its screen time; documents are slow).
- Documents the viewer can't read: if the text is illegible at video resolution, it's wallpaper — use a legible excerpt or don't use it.

## Narrative situation
Warnings (the ignored memo), reckoning (the report), legacy (the rule/law that changed).

## Visual sequence
1. The document full-frame or at a readable angle, held still 1–2 seconds.
2. Slow push-in to the relevant passage as the narration quotes or paraphrases it.
3. The key phrase highlighted or underlined (the annotation layer, minimal — one mark).
4. Hold on the marked passage while the implication lands. Then cut.

## Typical duration
8–15 seconds per document beat. The push-in should land on the passage exactly as the narration reaches the quoted words.

## Motion strategy
Slow push-in toward the passage. The document itself never moves otherwise — stillness *is* the credibility signal. Contrast with ARCHIVAL_PHOTO_MOVEMENT: documents get reverence, photos get survey.

## Transition strategy
Hard cut in; hold; hard cut out. No page-turn effects, no paper-texture transitions (restraint).

## Sound strategy
[RESEARCH GAP.] Conservative: silence or near-silence under document beats. Paper-rustle SFX is kitsch — never.

## Common mistakes
- Illegible documents (if the viewer can't read the passage, the beat fails — excerpt and enlarge).
- Document montages (five reports flashing past = zero credibility; one document held = evidence).
- Paraphrasing while showing unrelated paper (the words and the page must agree).
- Fake documents (never fabricate a memo/report visual — honesty gate; use the real page or a clearly-labeled reconstruction).

## Chain-of-Failure application
The warnings beat is built from documents: the ignored memo, the passed inspection, the vibration log. Each warning gets one document, one push-in, one highlighted passage. The reckoning beat is built the same way: the report's conclusion, quoted on screen. The viewer should feel the paper trail under the chain.

# Pattern: LEGACY_ENDING

## Purpose
Close every episode on what the chain changed — the rule, the redesign, the law — then remembrance. The legacy frame is the channel's signature and its subscription engine ("this is why your world works this way" [INFERENCE from structure]). Never end on spectacle.

## When to use
Every episode. The final 2–4 minutes: reckoning → legacy → remembrance → final line.

## When NOT to use
Never skip. If the failure genuinely changed nothing, say so — "nothing changed" is a legacy finding (and an indictment), not an excuse to end on the fire.

## Narrative situation
The close (template 14:00–18:00). The investigation has reported; now: what did it change, and who is remembered.

## Visual sequence
1. Reckoning: document close-ups — the report's conclusions on screen (DOCUMENT_EVIDENCE).
2. Legacy: present-day footage or diagrams of the *changed* system — the redesigned dam, the new rule in practice, the memorial. The visual rhyme: show the system *after* the chain.
3. Remembrance: the toll, stated plainly — names where the record supports it ("The Eleven" [OBSERVED]).
4. Final image: the changed system or the memorial. Held. Then end.

## Typical duration
2–4 minutes for the full close. The final line is one sentence; the final image holds 3–5 seconds.

## Motion strategy
Decelerate. The legacy close is the second-slowest passage after the catastrophe. Slow moves, long holds. The urgency is over; the meaning arrives.

## Transition strategy
Hard cuts, slower rhythm. No fade-to-black montages (sentimentality). The end screen follows the final image — no lingering.

## Sound strategy
[RESEARCH GAP.] Conservative: "a resolved, sober tone for the legacy/remembrance close — never triumphant, never maudlin" (creative report). No stingers on tolls. Ever.

## Common mistakes
- Ending on the disaster footage (the observed amateur move — the genre norm is legacy + remembrance).
- The triumphant close ("and now we're safe!") — the tone is sober resolution, not victory.
- Skipping remembrance (the toll stated without names/faces when the record supports naming them).
- The cliffhanger ending (the Bible forbids unearned cliffhangers — the episode resolves; the *next* chain is teased on the end screen, not in the narrative).

## Chain-of-Failure application
The final line states what the chain cost and what it changed, in one sentence (Bible §4.7). The legacy close is where the editorial promise pays off: the viewer assembled the chain with us, and now sees its consequence in their present. "Competence satisfaction" — the viewer leaves feeling they *understand* something — is the observed emotional engine of the strongest outliers.

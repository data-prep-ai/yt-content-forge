# Pattern: MECHANISM_CUTAWAY

## Purpose
Make the invisible visible: animate the internal mechanism of the failure — the valve sequence, the erosion path, the pressure wave — as a 3D or 2D cutaway. The visual climax of the video (Bible §6.5).

## When to use
- The hidden-mechanism beat: the failure happened *inside* something (a dam foundation, a blowout preventer, a hull, a reactor).
- When the narration must explain a process over time (fluid flow, crack propagation, pressure accumulation).
- Once per video, at the mechanism reveal — this is the climax, not a recurring device.

## When NOT to use
- More than once per video (a second cutaway dilutes the first; the climax must stay singular).
- When an annotated diagram suffices (static marks are cheaper and often clearer — see ANNOTATED_DIAGRAM).
- As spectacle: the cutaway explains, it doesn't thrill. No dramatic camera orbits for their own sake.

## Narrative situation
The hidden-mechanism beat — typically 25–40% runtime (Teton "Piping Failure" at 26% [OBSERVED]). Placed after the links are established and before the escalation: the viewer now understands *what the machine was doing wrong*.

## Visual sequence
1. Exterior establishing view of the structure (the dam, the rig, the ship) — oriented, labeled.
2. Cutaway transition: the exterior becomes transparent/sectioned (simple wipe or fade — the *reveal* is the effect, not the transition).
3. The mechanism animates through the failure sequence in sync with narration: water finding the path, the valve failing to close, the pressure building.
4. Freeze on the failed state with annotations (hands off to ANNOTATED_DIAGRAM).
5. Cut to the external consequence.

## Typical duration
45–120 seconds. The longest single visual beat in the video. Narration slows here (the Alertometer pairing: 134.6 WPM under continuous animation [OBSERVED]).

## Motion strategy
The mechanism moves; the camera stays nearly still. Slow rotation or section-shift only to reveal a new internal layer. Restraint: the *process* is the motion.

## Transition strategy
Simple fade or straight cut into the cutaway. No 3D fly-through transitions — the cutaway itself is the spectacle; the transition must be invisible.

## Sound strategy
[RESEARCH GAP.] Conservative: low drone under the sequence; silence or near-silence at the moment of failure *within* the cutaway. No mechanical SFX unless the mechanism's sound is documented evidence.

## Common mistakes
- Two cutaways (the second one tells the viewer the first wasn't the climax).
- Cutaway as eye candy: animating the *exterior* dramatically instead of the *mechanism* clearly.
- Rushing the narration over the cutaway (slow down — the visual is dense; the voice must give it room).
- Unexplained cutaway (every moving part must be labeled or narrated — an unlabeled animation is a screensaver).

## Chain-of-Failure application
The cutaway is the moment the chain becomes physical: the viewer *sees* the water moving through the foundation, the valve failing to seat, the crack running. Place it at the mechanism reveal, make it the video's visual climax, then never repeat it. Everything after the cutaway is consequence.

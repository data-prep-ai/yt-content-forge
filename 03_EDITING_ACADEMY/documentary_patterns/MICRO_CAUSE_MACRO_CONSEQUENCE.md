# Pattern: MICRO_CAUSE_MACRO_CONSEQUENCE

## Purpose
Name the tiny failure and the catastrophic price in the same breath. The single most portable breakout device measured (9/10 small-channel breakouts [OBSERVED]). In editing terms: the micro-cause gets its own visual beat, and the edit never lets the viewer forget the scale gap.

## When to use
- The title/thumbnail names a micro-cause ("a $10 valve", "a hand-tight flange", "a temporary pipe", "every corner cut") — the video must then *show* that cause as a discrete visual beat.
- Any chain link where a small decision has a disproportionate consequence.

## When NOT to use
When the record doesn't support a clean micro-cause (some failures are genuinely systemic with no single small trigger). Never manufacture one — the honesty gate forbids it.

## Narrative situation
The link beats (3:00–10:00 in the template). Each micro-cause is introduced as an ordinary object/decision, then the edit reveals its consequence.

## Visual sequence
1. The micro-cause shown at human scale: the valve, the flange, the memo, the inspection stamp — a tight, legible shot.
2. Annotated callout on the object (the "draw the failure" layer at minimum: a label, a circle).
3. Pull-back or cut to the macro-consequence: the scale of what followed (the flood, the wreckage, the toll number).
4. The gap between shot 1 and shot 3 is the pattern — ordinary object → catastrophe.

## Typical duration
30–90 seconds per micro-cause beat. The reveal (step 3) lands in under 5 seconds — the contrast must be abrupt, not gradual.

## Motion strategy
Slow push-in on the micro-cause (intimacy); hard cut to the consequence (shock of scale). No dissolve — the cut *is* the meaning.

## Transition strategy
Hard cut between the small and the large. The edit performs the disproportion.

## Sound strategy
[RESEARCH GAP.] Conservative: the micro-cause beat is quiet (the ordinariness is the point); no stinger on the consequence reveal — let the scale do the work.

## Common mistakes
- Showing the catastrophe first and the cause as an afterthought (inverts the device — the cause must be *felt* as small before the price lands).
- Over-explaining the micro-cause with jargon before showing it (show the object first, name it second).
- Using the device when the cause wasn't actually small (manufactured disproportion = honesty-gate failure).

## Chain-of-Failure application
Every link beat should be tested against this pattern: can the decision be shown as one ordinary object, one signature, one ignored reading? If yes, give it the micro-cause treatment. The chain is a sequence of small things; the edit must keep them feeling small until the price arrives.

# Pattern: PARALLEL_CASE_INTERLUDE

## Purpose
Insert a second, related failure mid-video as comparator or precedent. Trains the viewer to expect the channel's catalog to interconnect — the next video is already teased inside this one (retention/session device [INFERENCE from structure]).

## When to use
- When a genuine precedent or parallel exists in the record (Fontenelle's 1965 near-failure inside the Teton story — a documented organizational warning [OBSERVED]).
- Placed late (65–90%): as a re-hook (REF_001's Fontenelle at 88%) or mid-escalation as a comparator (Scandies Rose's F/V Destination at 22%).
- Maximum once per video.

## When NOT to use
- When the parallel is a stretch (the honesty gate: the comparison must be defensible, not decorative).
- More than once (two interludes = an anthology, not a chain).
- As the cold open (the parallel earns its place late, after the main chain is established).

## Narrative situation
Late-video re-hook or mid-escalation comparator. The interlude answers "was this unique?" or "has this happened before?"

## Visual sequence
1. Clear signal that we're leaving the main story: chapter title ("Fontenelle", "The Parallel"), possibly a distinct color treatment on the timeline.
2. Compressed chain of the parallel case: 60–120 seconds, same grammar (confident system → warning → failure) at high speed.
3. The comparative beat: what the parallel proves about the main chain (the warning that was ignored *twice*, the pattern).
4. Hard cut back to the main story — the viewer returns with new context.

## Typical duration
60–150 seconds. Compressed but complete — a mini-chain, not a montage.

## Motion strategy
Slightly faster cutting than the main story (it's a compressed telling). The visual language stays consistent — same annotation style, same restraint.

## Transition strategy
The chapter title is the transition. A brief orienting line ("Eleven years earlier, the same agency…") then the compressed chain. Hard cut back.

## Sound strategy
[RESEARCH GAP.] Conservative: same palette as the main video — the interlude is part of this video, not a different show.

## Common mistakes
- The parallel that isn't parallel (different failure mode, different era, no causal rhyme — the viewer feels the stretch).
- Letting the interlude run long (it's a beat, not a second video — cap at ~2 minutes).
- No comparative payoff (if the interlude doesn't change how the viewer sees the main chain, cut it).
- Using the interlude as the re-hook when the main story already has one (one re-hook per final third — don't stack them).

## Chain-of-Failure application
The interlude is the catalog-tease: "this chain rhymes with another chain." For our channel, the parallel should always be a *candidate future episode* — the interlude is the trailer the viewer doesn't know is a trailer. Maintain a list of used parallels to avoid repeating them as full episodes without acknowledgment.

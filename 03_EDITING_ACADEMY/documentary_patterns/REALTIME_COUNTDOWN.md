# Pattern: REALTIME_COUNTDOWN

## Purpose
Convert history into a vigil: tell the final minutes in real time, chaptered by the clock. The viewer signs up to sit through the catastrophe as it happened — unskipped dread as the retention engine.

## When to use
Subjects with timestamped final minutes: sinkings with radio logs, collapses with seismic/eyewitness times, explosions with minute-by-minute records. The record must support minute-level reconstruction — never invent timestamps.

## When NOT to use
- When the timeline is uncertain (reconstructing "real time" from a thin record is fabrication — use the standard template instead).
- For the full video: the countdown is a *variant block* (typically the escalation-to-catastrophe passage), not the whole structure. REF_003/012 still open with context and close with investigation.
- When the subject's power is the *chain* rather than the *minutes* (slow systemic failures need REWIND_AND_REBUILD, not a clock).

## Narrative situation
The escalation-to-catastrophe block (template 6:00–10:00 becomes the ticking clock). Chapters are clock-stamped; the narration follows the log.

## Visual sequence
1. The clock appears: an on-screen time graphic, persistent through the block (the one place a persistent graphic is earned).
2. Each chapter is a timestamp: "08:40 - 7:10 PM — 'We Are Holding Our Own'" [OBSERVED].
3. Visuals follow the log: positions, readings, the last report — map/diagram/archival in clock order.
4. "The Empty Radar Screen" beat: the moment contact is lost — held, near-silent [OBSERVED chapter 09:20].
5. "Gone in Seconds": the catastrophe inside the clock [OBSERVED chapter 10:00].

## Typical duration
3–6 minutes of clock time. Chapter density ≈ 1 per 35–40 seconds (REF_003: 24 chapters/15:47; REF_012: 31/18:17 [OBSERVED]).

## Motion strategy
The clock is the motion. Visuals stay in the established grammar (archival, map, diagram). The *timestamps* advance — that's the dynamism.

## Transition strategy
Chapter boundaries are timestamps — hard cuts on the clock. The cut *is* the tick.

## Sound strategy
[RESEARCH GAP.] Conservative: the clock needs no ticking SFX (kitsch). Near-silence at the "empty screen" beat — the observed silence-shaped moment.

## Common mistakes
- Inventing timestamps to fill the clock (every timestamp must come from the record — logs, transcripts, seismic data).
- The clock as decoration (if the chapters aren't truly sequential, it's not real-time — don't label it so).
- Countdown for the whole runtime (the context still needs the rewind; the vigil needs the investigation after).
- Title/thumbnail clock mismatch (REF_003's "29 MINUTES" vs "Last 17 Minutes" — sloppiness, not strategy).

## Chain-of-Failure application
The countdown variant compresses the chain's final links into the clock: each timestamp is a link (the visor falls → the list → the point of no return → she rolls). Use it when the record gives you the minutes; the chain still needs its rewind before and its reckoning after. The clock is the escalation block, not the video.

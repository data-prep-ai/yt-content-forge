# Pattern: TIMELINE_GRAPHIC

## Purpose
Make the chain visible as a graphic the viewer follows: links fill in as the narration reaches them, timestamps accumulate, the structure of the video becomes a structure on screen.

## When to use
- Introduced in the stakes section (0:20–1:00): the chain appears with links unfilled — the viewer holds the structure as an open loop.
- Returned to at each link: the current link fills/highlights.
- Countdown subjects: the timeline becomes the clock (see REALTIME_COUNTDOWN).

## When NOT to use
- As a persistent lower-third throughout (clutter — bring it up for orientation beats, then clear it).
- When the chain has more than ~7 links (a 12-link timeline is unreadable — group links into phases).

## Narrative situation
Stakes/promise (introduction), link transitions, escalation (filling), point of no return (the marker).

## Visual sequence
1. First appearance: horizontal chain graphic, links unfilled/labeled by phase ("The Decision", "The Warning", "The Failure").
2. Each link beat: the timeline returns briefly; the current link fills with a simple animation.
3. Point of no return: a distinct marker on the timeline (different color — the visual "past this point" line).
4. Final appearance: the complete chain, all links filled, held under the legacy narration.

## Typical duration
5–10 seconds per appearance. The timeline is orientation, not content — in and out.

## Motion strategy
Simple fills and highlights. The timeline builds; it doesn't dance.

## Transition strategy
Hard cut in/out. The timeline's appearances should feel like checking a map, not like a segment.

## Sound strategy
[RESEARCH GAP.] Conservative: no SFX on link fills.

## Relevant timestamps
- Template position: 0:20–1:00 (first appearance, links unfilled); each link beat; the point-of-no-return marker

## Common mistakes
- The timeline as wallpaper (persistent = ignored within 2 minutes).
- Too many links (unreadable — group into phases: Decisions / Warnings / Escalation / Failure).
- Filling links ahead of the narration (the timeline must never spoil the chain — it fills *with* the story, never before it).

## Chain-of-Failure application
This pattern is the visual identity of our editorial promise: the chain on screen, filling link by link, is the viewer *watching the thesis assemble*. The final filled chain under the legacy close is the signature image — "this is what connected."

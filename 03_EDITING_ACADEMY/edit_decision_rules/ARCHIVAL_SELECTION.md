# ARCHIVAL_SELECTION — which stills earn screen time

## The test
A still earns screen time if and only if it shows one of: the subject, the place, the people, the evidence, or the mechanism. "Atmosphere" alone is not enough — the genre's visual promise is evidence (Bible §7.2).

## Operational rules

- IF the photo shows the actual subject/place/people of the story → THEN use it, with Ken Burns movement (see ARCHIVAL_PHOTO_MOVEMENT).
- IF the photo is the only visual record of a key moment (the breach, the wreckage, the signing) → THEN give it a full beat: 4–8 seconds, slow push to the significant detail.
- IF the photo's significant detail is small in frame → THEN the Ken Burns move must end on that detail. The move is editorial: it tells the viewer where to look.
- IF a photo is low-resolution or damaged → THEN use it anyway if it's the primary record (authenticity beats polish — the genre rewards real artifacts). Do not "enhance" it into something it isn't.
- IF no photo exists for a beat that needs one → THEN do not stretch an unrelated photo to cover it. Use a diagram, a map, or a disclosed reconstruction instead. A wrong photo is worse than no photo.
- IF the photo shows victims in distress or gore → THEN do not use it. Restraint is the genre norm and the Bible's hard gate (Bible §7.5).

## Sourcing discipline

- Every archival image is sourced in the video's source list (the Hydraulic Record model: "archival aerial photography of the June 5, 1976 failure: U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, public domain" [OBSERVED]).
- Public-domain government photography (Bureau of Reclamation, NTSB, CSB, USGS, Library of Congress) is the preferred pool — authoritative and free.
- Newspaper press archives are the secondary pool (the Fascinating Horror model: Tampa Bay Times, LA Times [OBSERVED]).

## Anti-patterns

- The Ken Burns slideshow: every still getting the identical slow zoom regardless of content (vary direction; end on the detail the narration names).
- Static holds past ~8 seconds (dead air — the number-one amateur tell in photo-driven documentary) [production-model norm].
- Watermarked or licensing-unclear images (if the license can't be verified, the image doesn't ship).
- Cropping a photo to misrepresent its content (honesty gate applies to framing too).

## Evidence
Fascinating Horror's source lists are press archives and its thumbnails are archival photos (4.66M views on the photo-driven model [OBSERVED]). primary-source-history.md documents Ken Burns on photos/maps/documents as the standard cost-effective technique for this production model.
